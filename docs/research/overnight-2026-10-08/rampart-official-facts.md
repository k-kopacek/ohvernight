## 3. Dirt-bike legality

**Motorcycles and ATVs allowed in the area.**
- [ORI] FS-RR: "The Rampart Range Recreation Area provides a unique setting for off-highway vehicle (OHV) enthusiasts to enjoy a variety of riding experiences. From intermediate to expert riders, ATV or motorcycle, the Rampart has something for everyone."
- [ORI] FS-DF: "a system of 115 miles of motorcycle and ATV trails".
- [ORI] FS-FR: "ATVs and off-road motorcycles are allowed to enter and depart the campground for trail access. Consult the current motor vehicle use map for specific roads and trails open to off-road vehicles."

**Vehicles over 50 inches.**
- [ORI] FS-DF: "Only vehicles 50” or less in width can be on trails."
- [ORI] RECGOV: "motorized trail system (OHV's less than 50 inches in width)".

**Rampart Range Road (NFSR 300) on an unlicensed dirt bike: not legal.**
- [ORI] FS-DF, restrictions "apply to the Rampart Range Motorized Recreation Area": "A current license plate and valid driver’s license are required to ride on the roads." and, for trails, "A Colorado OHV (Off Highway Vehicle) registration or current license plate with OHV permit is required to ride on the trails."
- [P] RRMMC-DIR: "Note that Rampart Range Road is not open to unlicensed vehicles. The Powerline (#690) and beginner trails (#627) parallel the road most of the way and can be used to get from one area to another if your bike is not licensed."
- [D] Road rule and trail rule are different: trails need OHV registration; roads need a plate and a licensed driver. An unplated bike must be trucked to a staging area and ridden only on designated trails. It may not be ridden on Rampart Range Road between a staging area and the family's picnic spot.
- [ORI] FS-FR contains an ambiguous sentence: "Because this is the only designated campground allowing off-road vehicles on the road, it tends to fill quickly." [D] Read with the sentence quoted above, this concerns entering and leaving the campground for trail access; it is not a statement that NFSR 300 is open to unlicensed vehicles.

## 6. Food storage / bears, pets, hammock

**Food storage (applies to day visitors too).**
- [SF] ORD-FOOD, Order 02-12-00-23-07, "in effect from June 6, 2023 ... through June 5, 2028", on "All NFS lands located on the Pike-San Isabel National Forests ... which includes the Pikes Peak, South Platte ... Ranger Districts", prohibits: "Possessing or leaving unattended any food (items intended for human or domestic animal consumption), any refuse from the preparation, consumption, and cleanup of such items, or other bear attractants (such as bird feeders, cooking equipment, personal care products, and coolers), unless it is: Stored inside a hard-sided vehicle or camper. / Stored inside a securable container or vehicle constructed of a solid, non-pliable material ... / Suspended at least ten feet above the ground and four feet from any tree, post, or other vertical support. / Being eaten, prepared for eating, or transported in a motor vehicle. 36 C.F.R. § 261.58(cc)".
- [ORI] RECGOV: "Be bear aware; keep all food out of sight in approved containers or locked inside your vehicle and remove all food from area after eating".
- [D] A picnic is fine while the food is "being eaten, prepared for eating"; coolers, stoves and rubbish go back into the closed hard-sided vehicle when not in use, and must not be left on a table while the family walks off. An open pickup bed is not a "hard-sided vehicle" enclosure in the order's sense - my inference, not stated.

**Pets.**
- [ORI] FS-RR and FS-FR: "Dogs must be on a leash at all times."

**Rubbish.** [ORI] RECGOV "Pack in - Pack out"; FS-CR "Pack it in; pack it out."

**Hammock.**

Hammock use: Not established from reviewed sources.

- No reviewed Forest Service page, forest order, Recreation.gov record or RRMMC page mentions hammocks, or attaching anything to trees, for Flat Rocks Campground, the dispersed sites, the picnic areas or the trailheads. All retrieved page texts were searched for "hammock" and "tree".
- The only tree-related rule text found is in the food storage order and concerns hanging food: "Suspended at least ten feet above the ground and four feet from any tree, post, or other vertical support." (ORD-FOOD). It is not a hammock rule.
- Related current notice: "expect to see many downed and weakened trees ... even if a tree looks okay, it may be weakened and could fall" (ALERT-TREES), plus forecast gusts to 28 mph on Saturday (NWS). [D] This is a safety consideration for anything hung from trees; it is not a rule.

- Absence of a prohibition is not permission. The answer should say hammock use is not established and suggest asking the ranger district or the campground host.

## 7. Alternates

All reasons are [D], built from the facts above.

1. **Dutch Fred Trailhead (rider) + Cabin Ridge Picnic Area (family)** - choose this if Flat Rocks Campground turns out to be closed or unserviced this weekend, or if the rider wants the Dutch Fred trails. Cabin Ridge is a purpose-built day-use site showing "Site Open": 10 units with tables and fire rings, vault toilet, $7 (check or money order), no water, about 1 mile south of the Dutch Fred turn on the same road. Costs about 3-4 more road miles each way than Flat Rocks. The rider cannot ride the unplated bike on the road between the two; RRMMC lists a trailhead for trail 675 (Cabin Ridge Trail) at mile 8.1 and labels the picnic area "(Cabin Ridge TH)", but trail connectivity and vehicle class were not confirmed from the MVUM.
2. **Topaz Point Picnic Area (family) with staging at a nearby southern trailhead** - choose this for the quietest of the developed day-use options further from the busiest northern staging lots, and because the Forest Service page says the picnic season "continues into October". 10 sites, vault toilets, $7 (check or money order), no water. About 6 road miles past Flat Rocks. Nearest RRMMC-listed trailhead is mile 11.3 (trail 677, Log Jumper); nothing reviewed states its difficulty or facilities.
3. **Sunset Point Trailhead (mile 2.8) for the rider, family at Flat Rocks Campground day use (mile 4.5) or staying in town** - choose this for the shortest drive on the forest road and a partner-reported "Loading ramp, vault toilet". No picnic facilities are reported there and the rider and family would be about 1.7 road miles apart with no legal way to ride between them on the road.
4. **Sprucewood Inn area parking (about 3 miles past the Rampart Range Road turn on Highway 67)** - partner-reported "small parking area ... with access to some of the more difficult trails"; choose only for an experienced rider. Not on Forest Service pages reviewed; no family facilities established.

Primary candidate, for comparison: **Flat Rocks** (trailhead at mile 4.2, campground day use at mile 4.5, overlook and kiddy track at mile 4.6). It is the closest staging-plus-developed-family-site combination, the only one with potable water and the only one where the Forest Service says off-road motorcycles may enter and leave the site itself - but its stated season ended in late September and its status and water for 10-11 October are unverified.
