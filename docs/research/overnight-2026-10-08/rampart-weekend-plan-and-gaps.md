# PART 1 - THE ANSWER

## Best plan

**Unload the bike at Cabin Ridge Trailhead. The family spends the day at Cabin Ridge Picnic Area, a short walk or a two-minute drive away. Both are on Rampart Range Road, about 8.3 to 8.5 miles south of Highway 67.**

Bring a cheque for the $7 picnic fee, all your water, and do not ride the bike on any road.

## Why it fits

- It is the only pairing in the evidence where the rider's lot and a real picnic site are close enough to reunite on foot. The two official coordinates are about 290 m apart; by road it is about 0.2 mile along Rampart Range Road plus a 0.25 mile spur. [derived] L, F
- Cabin Ridge Picnic Area is a purpose-built day-use site: "ten (10) units with picnic tables, fire rings and a vault toilet", "Nestled in the woods". [official] F, O
- It does not depend on the campground season, which has ended (Flat Rocks). [official] F
- The trailhead sits on the main gravel road. It does not need the high-clearance spur that Dutch Fred does. [agency data] F
- The two trails at the trailhead (0675 and 0627) both have a clean row in the MVUM seasonal table. Dutch Fred's own trail (0679) is missing from that table. [controlling] L
- It did not win because it is closest. Flat Rocks is about 4 miles nearer and has water, but nothing online says the campground is open to day visitors this weekend.

**Thin evidence, stated plainly.** The trailhead-plus-picnic-area pairing is a late idea from one worker (F section 3.3), not something any source recommends. Nothing describes the trailhead lot's surface, unloading room or trailer room. No source states a footpath between the two sites. See "Where the evidence is too thin".

## 2.3 Implications by milestone

**M6 - trails, staging, motorized use, relationships**
- The legal answer lives in a PDF table that is graphics, not in the agency feature service the pipeline already ingests; the two disagree for about 40 Douglas segments. M6 needs a reviewed-claim path for map designations and must preserve the conflict, not compute over the layer.
- Relationships must carry their origin. This trip produced all four kinds at one lot: agency-stated (0675 at Cabin Ridge Trailhead), partner-stated (0681 at Dutch Fred), geometry-derived (0627 passing 1 m away), and refuted (0674 named for Flat Rocks but 0.8 km off). Proximity must never be promoted.
- Road rules are part of trail planning. A plated-versus-unplated rule cannot be derived from the agency vehicle-class fields; it needs a reviewed interpretation attached to a road identifier, with spur roads left unknown where the map contradicts itself.

**M7 - camping, day use, facility legality, current orders**
- Day use is a first-class need, not a by-product of camping. Picnic sites, campground day use, dispersed-site day use and trailhead lingering each came out differently (established, seasonal-unknown, not offered, not addressed).
- Orders are the controlling facts and none is machine-readable. Scope had to be worked out per site from an exhibit table, a township lookup and a map image. M7 needs order-to-site applicability as a reviewed claim with an expiry date.
- Status needs a time, not a flag. The database said OPEN for a campground three weeks past its published season. Published season end, undated flags and dated alerts must stay separate, and "open" must never be inferred from the first two.

**M8 - origin routing, trip assembly, ranking, requested unknowns**
- The unit of an answer is a pairing with a stated relationship (same site, walk of N, drive of N), its origin (source-backed or derived), and a legality test across the pair: here, "reunite without a road".
- Ranking must be able to put a farther option first and say why. The nearest candidate lost on seasonal reliability; the router's shortest route lost on evidence.
- The output must carry its own doubt: per-fact source class and Checked time, a list of requested unknowns the product could not answer (hammock, trailer), and the confirm-before-leaving steps. A plan that reads YES when the honest answer is MOSTLY would be the trust failure.
