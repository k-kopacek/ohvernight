# Customer discovery interview kit

**Summary.** This kit helps the owner interview real Colorado outdoor travellers about trips they actually took. It does not pitch Ohvernight and never asks "would you use this?". It contains a screener, consent and note rules, a 30-minute core script, eight segment plans (dispersed campers, hikers, anglers, paddlers, hunters, off-road users, RV/trailer users, multi-activity weekend travellers), the signals that would support or weaken each of the seven bets in `docs/research/product-opportunities/product-bets.md`, red flags for politeness, ten no-cost places to find people, a one-page synthesis template and a stopping rule. Nothing here is to be run, sent or published until the owner decides to. No business decision is made here.

Evidence labels: **HERMES-SOURCED** (stated in the research reports under `docs/research/product-opportunities/`), **VERIFIED** (read in a file), **INFERENCE** (my reasoning), **UNKNOWN**.

## 2. Ground rules for the interviewer

1. **Past behaviour only.** Ask "tell me about the last time", not "would you" or "do you think". Opinions about hypothetical products are worth little. INFERENCE (standard discovery practice).
2. **Do not show or describe Ohvernight.** If asked, say "I am researching how people plan trips; I will not demo anything today". This is a protocol choice so answers are not shaped by the product.
3. **Do not name your own preferred answer.** Never offer a list of options before the person has answered in their own words.
4. **Never imply that any place is legal or open.** If a participant asks "can I camp at X?", say you cannot advise and point them to the land manager. The interview is research, not a recommendation service. This follows the repository trust rule that existence never implies permission (`docs/product/trust-principles.md`).
5. **Do not take money, sell, or collect payment details.** Questions about price ask about money already spent, never about money they would spend.
6. **Silence is a tool.** Wait five seconds after an answer. People add the real detail then.

## 3. Recruiting screener

Use as a short form or a five-minute chat. Do not tell candidates what the "right" answers are.

| # | Question | Qualify if |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In the past 12 months, how many nights did you spend outdoors away from home (tent, camper, vehicle, backcountry)? | 3 or more |
| 2 | When was your most recent trip, and where? | Within the last 6 months, and the person can recall details |
| 3 | What did you do on that trip besides sleeping? (open answer) | At least one named activity |
| 4 | Did any part of that trip take place in Colorado? | Yes (at least four of the ten should be Colorado trips; Aspen and Douglas County area trips are a bonus, not a requirement) |
| 5 | Who made the plan? | They did or shared it |
| 6 | Which apps, websites or maps did you use? (open answer) | Names at least one |
| 7 | Do you pay for any outdoor app, map, licence or pass? | Record yes/no and which; do not exclude non-payers, you need both |
| 8 | Do you work for an outdoor app, land agency, outfitter or media outlet? | No (exclude, or interview separately as an expert) |
| 9 | Which best describes you? (single choice) | Used to place into a segment plan in section 5 |

Quota guidance for the first ten: no more than three from any one segment, at least three who pay for one or more outdoor subscriptions, at least three who pay for none, at least two who have had a trip fail or change last minute. INFERENCE.

## 4. Consent, privacy and note-taking

**Consent script (read aloud, or send in writing before the call):**

> I am doing independent research on how people plan outdoor trips. It takes about 30 minutes. I will take notes. If you agree I will also record audio, only so I can check my notes; the recording stays with me and is deleted after I write up. Your name will not appear in anything I publish or share. You can skip any question or stop at any time. I will not sell anything, and I am not asking for payment or personal contact details beyond how to reach you for a thank-you. Is that all right?

Rules:

- Get a spoken or written yes before recording. Note it. If they decline, take notes only.
- Do not collect exact home addresses, exact secret fishing, hunting or camping spots, licence numbers, plate numbers, or photographs of people. If a participant volunteers an exact favourite spot, write "named a specific spot" and generalise to a drainage or county. This is important for location privacy and aligns with the repository rule against storing raw personal contact details in published data (`AGENTS.md`, working rules). VERIFIED.
- Store notes in a private place under a participant code (P01, P02) with the code mapping kept separately. Do not paste notes into the repository.
- If you give a thank-you, make it a fixed small gift independent of what they say. Do not tie it to purchase or product use.
- Note-taking format: two columns per trip step, "What they did / said" in their words, and "My interpretation" kept separate. Quote exact phrases for frustrations and for anything about money.
- Mark each statement **B** (behaviour, in the past, specific), **O** (opinion), or **H** (hypothetical). Only B counts as evidence.

## 5. Core 30-minute script

Times are guides. Adjust follow-ups to what they say; the aim is a step-by-step reconstruction.

| Min | Block | Questions and prompts |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Warm-up and consent | Consent script. "Tell me a little about how you spend time outdoors." |
| 3-8 | Pick the last real trip | "Think about your most recent overnight trip. Where was it, who went, how long?" "What made you choose that place, that weekend?" "Where were you when you first started planning, and what was the first thing you opened?" |
| 8-18 | Walk the trip step by step | "Take me from that first moment until you were driving home. What happened first, then next?" For each step: "What were you looking at, on what device? What were you trying to find out? Did you find it? What did you do next?" Probe the order of apps and sites: "After that, what did you open?" Keep a numbered list of every tool in order. "How long did all the planning take, in total, across how many days?" "When you decided where to sleep, how did you know you were allowed to sleep there?" "How did you get to the start of the activity, and did you check the road or the entrance in advance?" |
| 18-23 | What failed, changed or surprised | "What went wrong, or was different from what you expected?" "Was there anything you found out only after you arrived?" "Did you change any part of the plan at the last minute? What triggered it, what did you use to decide, and from where?" "What information did you not fully trust? How did you check it? What did you end up believing?" |
| 23-27 | Money and tools | "Which apps, maps, memberships or passes are you paying for today?" For each: "When did you first pay? What was happening in your life or your trip at that moment? What had you tried before? Have you cancelled or thought about cancelling anything? Why?" "Did you pay for anything for this specific trip, such as a pass, a permit, a guidebook, a reservation fee, a map? What did it cost?" "Thinking of that trip, what was the one thing you would have paid money or time to avoid?" |
| 27-30 | Close | "What was the single most frustrating part of planning that trip?" "Who else do I know who plans trips like this?" (referral, not a pitch). Thank them. Confirm deletion of audio after write-up. |

**Do not ask:** "Would you use a tool that...?", "Would you pay $X?", "How much would you pay?", "Do you think an app showing... would help?", "Which of these features do you want?". Replace with the past-behaviour version in the right-hand column.

| Instead of | Ask |
|---|---|
| Would you pay for alerts? | Have you ever paid for an alert or notification of any kind? When? What happened right before? |
| Would offline maps matter? | What did you do the last time you had no signal? |
| Would you trust a confidence score? | Tell me about the last time you saw conflicting information about a place. What did you do? |
| Is a campsite finder useful? | Walk me through how you found the place you slept on your last trip. |

## 6. Segment plans

Each plan is added to the core script, replacing or deepening minutes 8-23. Pick one plan per interview; for multi-activity people use plan H. Probes assume the participant has already named a real trip.

### A. Dispersed campers

Persona link: weekend camper and 4x4 dispersed camper (`personas-and-monetization.md`, ranks 1 and 3). HERMES-SOURCED.

Segment probes:

- How did you decide which stretch of forest or BLM land to look at, and how did you know camping was allowed there? What source finally convinced you?
- Did you open a Motor Vehicle Use Map, a ranger page or a phone call? In what order relative to a map app?
- How did you find the exact spot? Satellite imagery, a crowd pin, a friend, driving around? How did you check the pin was not out of date?
- Stay limits, fire restrictions and food-storage orders: when and how did you find those out, if at all?
- What did you do when the spot was full or unusable? Was there a backup?
- Tell me about the last time a spot was not what you expected.

Support/weaken signals: see section 7, bets 2, 3, 1.

### B. Hikers

Segment probes:

- Where did you find the trail? How did you decide on that one?
- Where did you sleep the night before and after, and did you decide that before or after you picked the trail?
- How did you find the trailhead and parking? Did you check for fees, permits, timed entry, seasonal closures or snow?
- How did you check conditions in the days before? Which source did you trust most and least?
- Did you use a navigation app on the day? What did you do when the signal dropped?
- Did a closure, fire or snow ever change your plan? What did you do?

Hikers are likely the largest group and the least distinct; look for whether lodging is a separate hunt for them. INFERENCE.

### C. Anglers

Persona link: angler who camps near fishing access (rank 2). HERMES-SOURCED.

Segment probes:

- How did you learn whether you were allowed to fish that water? What did you check: regulations booklet, an agency page, signs, a fishing app, a shop, a friend?
- How did you work out where you could actually reach the bank or put in a boat? Public access point, easement, private land next to it?
- How did you know whether the stretch was closed, restricted (gear, bait, catch-and-release) or affected by flows or fire?
- Do you pay for a licence, a map, a fishing app or a guide? What triggered each purchase?
- Early starts: where did you sleep, and how far from the water? How did you choose?
- Tell me about a time you arrived at the water and could not fish or reach it.

### D. Paddlers (kayak, canoe, paddleboard, rafting)

Weakest direct evidence among water segments; treat as exploratory. HERMES-SOURCED (thin sample).

Segment probes:

- How did you find out whether paddling was allowed on that lake or reach, and by what type of craft?
- Boat inspection, permits, launch fees, hours: how and when did you find out?
- How did you find a launch and parking? Did you check water level, wind or flow?
- Where did you sleep, and was the overnight decided before or after the water? How far from the launch?
- Did anything about the launch, ramp or water body change on the day?

### E. Hunters

Not a ranked persona in the reports. UNKNOWN demand. Note: do not ask about exact hunting spots; accept a region or unit only. A hunting trip involves licences, seasons, game management units, and often private land and camp conditions; a mistaken product claim here could carry high consequence. INFERENCE.

Segment probes:

- How did you pick the unit or area? What did you consult (agency maps, an app, a landowner, a friend)?
- How did you check where public land stopped and private began, and how sure were you?
- Where did you camp: dispersed, campground, a vehicle? How did you know that was allowed during the season? Any stay limits or closures during the season?
- Road and gate access: how did you learn whether a road was open or gated that week?
- Do you pay for any land-ownership or mapping subscription? What triggered the first purchase?
- Tell me about the last time you found out you were in the wrong place or were turned back.

### F. Off-road users (4x4, overlanders, UTV and dirt bike riders)

Persona link: 4x4 dispersed camper or overlander (rank 3). HERMES-SOURCED.

Segment probes:

- Seasonal gates and closures: how did you find out whether a road was open on your date? What was the last gate you met that you did not expect?
- Vehicle class: how did you decide that your vehicle (stock, lifted, trailer, bike) could do the route? Where did you get difficulty information and how did you check it?
- Legal designation: did you consult a Motor Vehicle Use Map or an app? How did you treat the line between legal and passable?
- Where did you camp, and how did you know a spot off the road was allowed?
- What do you pay for now (onX, Gaia, another)? What triggered each purchase and what nearly made you cancel?
- Tell me about a time a road was worse than the source said.

### G. RV and trailer users

Persona link: RV or travel-trailer traveller (rank 4). HERMES-SOURCED.

Segment probes:

- Length, height and weight limits: how did you learn whether your rig fits a campground, road or pass? What did you check, and what did you simply risk?
- Turnarounds, tight corners, steep grades, narrow bridges: how did you find out about these before arriving? What happened when you found out on arrival?
- Reservation or first-come: how did you secure a site, and what did you do when it fell through?
- Fixed dates: what is your fallback if the plan fails while you are there?
- Hookups, water, dump stations, cell coverage: how did you check each?
- Subscriptions: which do you pay for (campground apps, route planners), what triggered them, which have you dropped?

### H. Multi-activity weekend travellers

Persona link: activity-first weekend camper (rank 1). HERMES-SOURCED.

Segment probes:

- You did more than one thing on that trip. How did you decide which came first, and how did you decide where to sleep so both worked?
- Which came first in planning, the activity or the place to sleep? Did that change during the weekend?
- How did you connect the sleep spot to each activity (driving time, trailhead, launch)? What tool did you use for that link?
- What did you give up to make the pieces fit?
- Count apps and sites you opened. Which were you opening again and again?
- What was the most annoying thing to reconcile between two tools?

## 7. Signals mapped to the seven bets

Success thresholds for the bets themselves are in `product-bets.md`. These are interview signals only, not proof. A single interview proves nothing; count patterns across interviews (section 11). Tags: **B** behaviour only.

| Bet | Supports (past behaviour) | Weakens (past behaviour) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Overnight + activity pairing | Activity chosen first and lodging hunted separately in a different tool; three or more tools in the planning chain; proximity gone wrong ("it was close but not allowed or not reachable"); lost an hour or more joining the two | Lodging fixed first (reservation, home base) and activities chosen afterwards; repeat visits to the same place with no new planning; one tool already does the join |
| 2. Evidence-backed access confidence | Opened an agency page or called a ranger to check permission; said "I did not trust the pin or the shading"; had a permit, ticket or turn-back; can name the specific source they trust and why | Never checked permission; "I just go"; trusts a crowd pin without caveat and had no bad outcome; cannot recall any check |
| 3. Freshness ("when was this checked?") | Found stale information and acted on it; looks for dates on reviews or notices; distrusts old pins; checked a closure or fire page shortly before leaving | Does not look at dates; relies on a friend or local shop for current conditions; has never found stale data |
| 4. Adventure matching | Reconstructed a combined plan from three or more tools; compared two or more candidate plans against dates, vehicle and activity; accepted giving up something to make it fit | Only ever goes to one or two familiar places; chooses on mood or word of mouth; a single tool already ranks what they need |
| 5. Saved-trip monitoring | Rechecked the same page or pages in the days before the trip; got caught by a change after planning; has paid for or turned on an alert before; plans 1-3 weeks ahead | Plans the day before; never rechecks; ignores or mutes alerts; changes were not a problem |
| 6. Trip feasibility dashboard | Describes assembling a go/no-go from several sources just before leaving (weather, snow, closures, fire, road); has made a late go/no-go decision | Decides on the day by feel; trusts a single source; reads no conditions |
| 7. Offline trip package | Lost signal and could not find a rule, road or reservation; printed or screenshotted information before leaving; pays for offline maps today and can say what happened that triggered it | Always has signal; never prepared offline; says offline is "nice" but cannot recall a case where it mattered |

Willingness-to-pay signal (applies to all bets): money already paid, the trigger event, the amount, whether they renewed. A stated price for a hypothetical product is not a signal. HERMES-SOURCED context: paid precedents exist for offline, alerts, vehicle tools and route planning; payment for evidence quality alone is UNKNOWN.

## 8. Red flags: politeness, not behaviour

Stop and re-ask in a past-behaviour form when you hear:

- "That sounds great", "I would totally use that", "that would be so helpful" (praise with no past trip attached).
- "I always check everything first" with no example, no source, no date.
- "Most people would..." (they are talking about other people).
- "I would pay for that" or a quick, round price with no past purchase.
- Answers that exactly mirror words you used in the previous question.
- A tidy, ideal workflow with no mess, no wrong turn, no abandoned tab (real planning is messy).
- Agreeing with every statement you offer.
- Inability to name a specific app, date or place from the last trip.
- Hesitation to criticise a tool you know they use.

Counter-moves: "When was the last time that happened? Tell me about it." "What did you actually do?" "Which tab was open?" "What did you pay last year for anything similar?" Mark any answer you could not anchor in a past event as **O** or **H** and give it zero weight in the tally.

All are free methods, and each needs the owner's own judgement about community rules. Do not post in communities that prohibit research requests; ask moderators first. Do not scrape or collect names. Do not impersonate a business. INFERENCE for all channel suggestions; no channel was tested.

| # | Place | Segments | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personal network and referrals ("who else plans trips like this") | All | Ask each interviewee for one referral at the close |
| 2 | Colorado outdoor subreddits (for example camping, hiking, fishing and overlanding communities named in `hermes/p2-customer-pain-and-workflows.md`) | A, B, C, F | Message moderators for permission to post a short request; offer a small fixed thank-you |
| 3 | Local fly shops and tackle shops | C | Ask the owner to let you leave a note or introduce one regular |
| 4 | Paddling clubs and outfitters, boat ramps in the off-peak | D | Ask the club newsletter or board for an introduction |
| 5 | Local 4x4 and overlanding clubs (Colorado-based groups and meetups) | F | Ask an officer to share a short request at a meeting or in the group |
| 6 | RV park and campground hosts, camp hosts at public campgrounds | G, A | Visit in person in the off-season; ask hosts to introduce long-stay campers |
| 7 | Trailhead conversations and trail-maintenance volunteer days | B, H | Ask in person after the activity; keep to a polite request, not a pitch |
| 8 | Public Facebook groups for Colorado camping or hunting (those that allow research requests) | A, E, H | Follow the group rules; do not message people unprompted |
| 9 | Local gear shop staff and climbing or hiking club meetups | B, H | Ask staff who the regulars planning big weekends are |

## 11. How many interviews before a conclusion is reasonable

INFERENCE (qualitative practice; no figure in the reports). Treat all of this as a judgement call for the owner.

| Question | Interviews |
|---|---|
| "Is the planning fragmentation we read about real in our own people?" | 5 to 8, mixed segments. If five of eight describe three or more tools and a trust failure, the pain is present |
| "Does one segment feel the pain more than the others?" | 5 to 6 in each segment you want to compare; fewer than that supports only a hunch |
| Direction on a specific bet | 8 to 10 in the target segment, with the same pattern in at least 6 of them |
| Anything about price | Interviews cannot settle this. They show past spend and triggers. Only a pricing test can show choice; see `mvp-experiments.md` |
| Representative claims about "Colorado outdoor people" | Not reachable by interviews; would need a survey of a few hundred |

Stopping rule: stop a segment when the last three interviews added no new tool, no new failure mode and no new trust behaviour. Do not stop before eight total even if the early ones are exciting, because early recruits are the most enthusiastic and least typical. If ten interviews show that most people have no trust-related trouble, that is a valid finding and should be recorded as weakening Bets 2 and 3.
