## Executive finding

The strongest bear case is not that outdoor recreation lacks users. Participation is large: the Outdoor Industry Association reported 175.8 million US participants in 2023, although outings per participant fell 11.4% year over year. The problem is converting infrequent, seasonal planning into recurring revenue while maintaining permission-sensitive data against free government products and much larger incumbents. **PRIMARY SOURCE.**  
https://outdoorindustry.org/press-release/outdoor-participation-hits-record-levels-for-ninth-consecutive-year/

Ohvernight’s thesis is not disproved, but its proposed breadth is. A small company probably cannot sustainably become a general-purpose activities-plus-overnight planner while also maintaining authoritative access status. The viable version, if any, is a narrow decision-support layer that sends users to operators for permission and transactions, concentrates on a recurring Colorado use case, and charges for a result unavailable from free sources—not for another map.

The evidence does **not** establish that users dislike switching apps enough to pay to stop. It does establish that integrated planners can attract use, but integration alone has not protected products from dormancy, acquisition, consolidation, or strategic abandonment.

**SERIOUS.** Integration can attract users, but the evidence does not show that planning integration by itself is a durable paid value proposition.

- Payment must attach to a scarce, measurable outcome: fewer failed trips, verified operator links, saved drive time, multi-activity compatibility, or a trustworthy “not established” answer.
- The paid product must retain value after the trip is planned.

Sell a concierge “Colorado overnight activity match” before building more software. Charge three price points—such as $9, $19, and $39—to randomized landing-page cohorts. Deliver briefs manually. ## (d) Authoritative-data maintenance is too expensive for a small team

### Strongest evidence for the bear view

- USGS retired the National Hydrography Dataset on October 1, 2023. The data remain available but are no longer maintained; users are directed to the 3D Hydrography Program. This is direct evidence that even foundational government datasets change programs and maintenance status. **PRIMARY SOURCE.**  
  https://www.usgs.gov/national-hydrography/national-hydrography-dataset

- Gaia GPS removed Esri World Imagery in March 2025 because Esri had “significantly increased their licensing costs,” which Gaia called unsustainable. A scaled incumbent could not economically preserve one familiar layer. **PRIMARY SOURCE.**  
  https://blog.gaiagps.com/gaia-gps-is-improving-satellite-imagery-saying-goodbye-to-esri-world-imagery/

- Pew found that 21% of sampled government webpages contained at least one broken link and 38% of webpages from 2013 were inaccessible by 2023. A provenance product built on URLs therefore needs persistent checking, replacement, and historical context. **PRIMARY SOURCE.**  
  https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2024/05/17/when-online-content-disappears/

- COTREX aggregates more than 230 trail managers and says more than 36 partners post real-time advisories. Even with that institutional network, CPW warns that information “can still vary in accuracy and timeliness.” **PRIMARY SOURCE.**  
  https://cpw.state.co.us/cpw-apps  
  https://cpw.state.co.us/news/12042025/colorado-trail-explorer-cotrex-app-shares-trail-closures-protect-wintering-wildlife

### Evidence against

- Ohvernight is static and geographically bounded. It can link to source pages and expose timestamps rather than replicate every agency dataset. This is an **INFERENCE** about architecture, not evidence that maintenance is cheap.

- COTREX provides a high-quality Colorado activity layer for free. If Ohvernight treats it as a source to consult rather than recreating it, some maintenance burden is avoidable. COTREX still does not establish overnight permission outside what an operator expressly states. **PRIMARY SOURCE plus trust-rule limitation.**  
  https://cpw.state.co.us/news/12042025/colorado-trail-explorer-cotrex-app-shares-trail-closures-protect-wintering-wildlife

### How serious

**SERIOUS and capable of becoming fatal as geographic or activity scope expands.**

### What would have to be true for Ohvernight to survive it

- It must maintain a small set of high-value claims rather than ingest everything.
- Every permission-sensitive record needs publisher, source URL, retrieval date, quoted basis, geographic scope, season, and an explicit unknown state.
- Broken-link monitoring and stale-record expiration must be automatic.
- Human review must focus first on restrictions, closures, and changes in permission.

### Cheapest way to test

Create a 100-record pilot drawn from Aspen and Douglas County. Check every source weekly for 12 weeks. Record URL failures, changed wording, changed dates, conflicting agencies, and review minutes. Use the observed labor per record to calculate the maximum affordable catalog size before expanding.

## (g) Access uncertainty makes recommendations too risky

### Strongest evidence for the bear view

- CPW says COTREX closure information comes from public sources but “can still vary in accuracy and timeliness.” CPW also says displayed seasonal closures are mandatory and enforceable. This demonstrates both the consequence of stale status and the limits of an authoritative statewide platform. **PRIMARY SOURCE.**  
  https://cpw.state.co.us/news/12042025/colorado-trail-explorer-cotrex-app-shares-trail-closures-protect-wintering-wildlife

- The Tenth Circuit’s 2025 *Iron Bar Holdings v. Cape* decision upheld corner crossing under the federal Unlawful Inclosures Act when hunters did not physically touch private land. The years of criminal and civil proceedings show that a map’s parcel geometry does not settle lawful access. **PRIMARY SOURCE.**  
  https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca10/23-8043/23-8043-2025-03-18.html

- In a 2025 Wyoming rescue, two hikers said AllTrails led them into a boulder field. AllTrails disputed that its route was inaccurate and said the listing warned of off-trail scrambling and scree. The incident is evidence of attribution and expectation risk, not proof that AllTrails caused the rescue. **PRIMARY SOURCE for the news report; causation UNKNOWN.**  
  https://cowboystatedaily.com/2025/09/29/hikers-rescued-from-medicine-bow-peak-say-gps-app-led-them-astray/

- Appalachian Trail managers interviewed by Virginia Tech researchers reported overcrowding, misinformation, trail damage, and users repeating illegal or inappropriate campsites shared through apps. The study involved 18 managers, so it is qualitative and should not be generalized to every platform. **PRIMARY SOURCE.**  
  https://news.vt.edu/articles/2025/03/clahs-trail-technology-research.html

- Research across 44 Oregon state parks found no average visitation effect from Instagram, but did find a positive association at highly engaged locations such as Smith Rock. Digital discovery may concentrate use at a small number of iconic sites rather than raise visitation everywhere. **PRIMARY SOURCE.**  
  https://le.uwpress.org/content/wple/early/2023/09/14/le.100.2.122920-0192R1.full.pdf

### Evidence against

- The AllTrails incident also demonstrates that clear difficulty and hazard notices can rebut a simplistic claim that the app recommended an ordinary maintained trail. Provenance and explicit limitations can reduce, though not eliminate, expectation risk. **INFERENCE.**  
  https://cowboystatedaily.com/2025/09/29/hikers-rescued-from-medicine-bow-peak-say-gps-app-led-them-astray/

- AllTrails reported to Maryland officials that targeted closure and warning alerts at Olympic National Park coincided with a 66% reduction in search-and-rescue incidents on historically high-incident trails. Because this is an AllTrails presentation rather than an independently retrieved evaluation, the causal claim is not established. **PRIMARY SOURCE for company claim; causation UNKNOWN.**  
  https://dnr.maryland.gov/Pages/summit/documents/AllTrails-MD-Outdoor-Summit-Presentation.pdf

- A system that refuses to infer permission and prominently reports unknowns may be safer than community lists or unlabeled map features. Whether users understand and follow those distinctions is **UNKNOWN**.

### How serious

**SERIOUS and potentially fatal if Ohvernight presents “recommendations” as permission or current safety advice.**

### What would have to be true for Ohvernight to survive it

- No positive access status may be inferred from ownership, trail existence, roads, facilities, stocking, map labels, or community use.
- Restrictions and closures must appear before attractions.
- Permission-sensitive statements must quote the responsible agency or operator, include a retrieval date, and expire.
- “Unknown” must block confident recommendation language.
- The product must distinguish discovery, permission, conditions, and safety as separate questions.
- Formal legal review and adequate insurance would be required before marketing the service as authoritative or personalized.

### Cheapest way to test

Give 20 users prototype cards containing “confirmed,” “restricted,” and “unknown” examples. Ask what they believe they may legally do, without prompting. If users interpret “nearby,” “public,” or “mapped” as permission despite warnings, the interface is unsafe and must be redesigned before launch.

## Assumptions that must be validated before more is built

1. A specific Colorado cohort plans at least several activity-plus-overnight trips per year.
2. That cohort currently uses multiple sources, and switching causes measurable delay, failed decisions, or abandoned trips.
3. Users will make a real payment for synthesis before receiving the result.
4. The valuable result is planning and permission provenance—not offline mapping.
5. Aspen and Douglas County contain enough operator-confirmed options to produce useful results without implying permission from map features.
6. Unknown and restricted results remain useful enough that honesty does not collapse conversion.
7. Users correctly understand that “near,” “public,” “mapped,” and “listed” do not mean allowed.
8. A 100-record corpus can be kept current at an economically tolerable labor cost.
9. Search demand exists for narrow combinations that incumbents do not already answer.
10. Repeat purchase or renewal survives across seasons.
11. Per-trip, seasonal, affiliate, or B2B revenue can outperform a generic annual consumer subscription.
12. A defensible liability posture is available after legal and insurance review.

## Recommendation

**Do not proceed with Ohvernight as a broad paid outdoor map or all-in-one planner.** That version faces free government substitutes, incumbent bundles, expensive data upkeep, weak evidence of paid switching pain, seasonal usage, and serious access-risk semantics.

Proceed only with a short, paid-validation phase for a narrower product: an evidence-first Colorado trip decision service that links selected activities to operator-confirmed overnight options, places restrictions first, labels unknowns prominently, and exports users to existing reservation and navigation tools.

Set hard kill criteria before more product work:

- fewer than 10% of qualified prospects pay for a manually produced trip result;
- fewer than 20% of purchasers return for a second trip within the relevant season;
- more than 15 minutes per record per month is required to keep permission-sensitive evidence current;
- users repeatedly interpret proximity, public ownership, or map presence as permission;
- free incumbents complete most pilot tasks without material failure;
- no low-cost search or partner channel produces customers at an acquisition cost supportable by observed revenue.

The honest judgment is **serious risk, not a disproven opportunity**. The market supports paid outdoor products, but the available evidence favors focused navigation, safety, and high-frequency utility—not integration for its own sake. Ohvernight should earn the right to continue by proving paid demand for one narrow Colorado decision before expanding data, geography, activities, or software.
