## Executive comparison

| Product | Primary strategic advantage | Primary structural weakness for Ohvernight’s problem |
|---|---|---|
| onX | High-value land-access, ownership, offline, and activity-specific mapping at national scale | Strong map context does not equal permission; camping, trail, road, and access evidence remain distributed across datasets and agencies |
| Gaia GPS | Extremely broad map-source catalogue, layering, offline maps, and international coverage | More a configurable map platform than an evidence-led place-to-stay decision system |
| AllTrails | Mainstream trail discovery, polished curation, large community, land-manager relationships, and simple navigation | Trail discovery is much stronger than overnight legality, provenance transparency, land-access reasoning, or cross-domain lodging |
| CalTopo | Serious planning, printable maps, high-resolution terrain, GIS layers, and collaborative workspaces | Powerful but specialist; not designed around consumer discovery, overnight choices, or permission explanations |
| Trailforks | Deep trail-network/community data, local moderation, conditions, popularity, and trail-association tooling | Activity-centric and mountain-bike-rooted; public trail visibility is not proof of legal access or camping permission |
| FarOut | Curated, guidebook-like, offline route guides for long-distance journeys | Route-guide coverage is selective and purchased guide by guide or through a subscription; weak for broad regional overnight discovery |
| COTREX | Colorado-specific authoritative trail-manager coordination and statewide coverage | Trails-first repository; its own terms expressly require users to consult land managers about legality and current conditions |

- PRIMARY SOURCE — onX describes its landlocked-public-land analysis as using county, state, and federal agency data, cross-referenced and refined by onX. https://www.onxmaps.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/onX_TRCP_West_Federal_Landlocked_Report.pdf

### Restrictions and permission honesty

- PRIMARY SOURCE — onX’s own landlocked-land report recognizes that public ownership does not itself create access where private permission or an easement is required. https://www.onxmaps.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/onX_TRCP_West_Federal_Landlocked_Report.pdf
- PRIMARY SOURCE — onX’s terms state that its content is not a legal survey and that definitive property descriptions should come from government records. https://www.onxmaps.com/tou
- INFERENCE — onX is materially more honest about the distinction between ownership and access than a simple public-land overlay, but users still need the responsible agency or landowner for current permission, closure, permit, and camping rules. https://www.onxmaps.com/tou

### What they do better than a small evidence-first regional map today

- INFERENCE — onX delivers a mature national-scale map system across hunting, motorized travel, and human-powered recreation, with offline maps, GPS tracking, land ownership, activity-specific layers, and guidebook content. A small Colorado map would be unlikely to match that breadth immediately. https://www.onxmaps.com/about
- INFERENCE — The separate-app strategy lets onX tune data, terminology, and paid products to distinct activities instead of forcing one generic recreation model. https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/app/pricing https://www.onxmaps.com/offroad/app/pricing https://www.onxmaps.com/backcountry/app/pricing
- INFERENCE — onX’s strongest immediate advantage over Ohvernight would be the combination of parcel-scale ownership, offline operation, and national coverage. https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/blog/onx-private-property-ownership-how-and-when-is-it-updated

### What they do worse

- INFERENCE — onX’s user-facing products combine ownership, roads, trails, campsites, and activity layers, but the retrieved pages do not show a uniform claim-level provenance panel for each mapped feature. https://www.onxmaps.com/backcountry/app/features
- INFERENCE — Its “possible access” and road/access analysis helps users find questions, not conclusively resolve permission. That leaves a gap for an evidence-first regional product that shows the governing agency, exact rule, source date, and unresolved status together. https://www.onxmaps.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/onX_TRCP_West_Federal_Landlocked_Report.pdf
- INFERENCE — onX’s separate activity products can fragment a trip that combines hiking, water, camping, roads, and lodging. https://www.onxmaps.com/about

### What they would be hard to beat at, and why

- INFERENCE — National parcel and access analysis would be hard to beat because onX combines many government and commercial sources with in-house verification and a long-running mapping operation. https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/blog/onx-private-property-ownership-how-and-when-is-it-updated
- INFERENCE — Offline map delivery and activity-specific navigation would be hard to beat because offline use is built into the paid product model and supported across several mature applications. https://www.onxmaps.com/offroad/app/pricing https://www.onxmaps.com/backcountry/app/pricing
- INFERENCE — Partnerships, acquisitions, and capital investment give onX more ability than a regional newcomer to add adjacent activity datasets and paid services. https://www.onxmaps.com/blog/onx-acquires-troutroutes https://www.onxmaps.com/news

### What not to copy

- INFERENCE — Do not copy the implication that a sophisticated access layer can replace an agency’s current rule, easement record, closure notice, or landowner permission. onX’s own report warns that road data does not establish access. https://www.onxmaps.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/onX_TRCP_West_Federal_Landlocked_Report.pdf
- INFERENCE — Do not begin with three or four activity-specific apps. A regional product should make the overnight decision flow across activities rather than make users assemble separate subscriptions. https://www.onxmaps.com/about
- INFERENCE — Do not make nationwide breadth the initial objective if it weakens source quality and update accountability in Aspen and Douglas County. https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/blog/onx-private-property-ownership-how-and-when-is-it-updated

### What their users still need another app for

- INFERENCE — Users still need official agency and operator pages for definitive camping permission, reservations, permits, seasonal closures, and site-specific rules. onX itself distinguishes map information from definitive records. https://www.onxmaps.com/tou
- INFERENCE — Users may need a lodging or reservation service to book cabins, campgrounds, private stays, or commercial overnight facilities; the retrieved onX pages describe recreation points but not a unified booking workflow. https://www.onxmaps.com/backcountry/app/features

### What feature could make a user switch

- INFERENCE — A Colorado trip planner that starts with chosen activities, filters only by source-supported overnight options, and presents a source-backed permission chain could make some users switch for trip planning while leaving onX as their field-navigation tool. https://www.onxmaps.com/tou

### What could make a user pay for both

- INFERENCE — onX’s field navigation plus Ohvernight’s regional overnight evidence would be complementary if Ohvernight links every overnight recommendation to the responsible agency/operator and clearly marks unresolved access. https://www.onxmaps.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/onX_TRCP_West_Federal_Landlocked_Report.pdf

### What could eventually replace them

- INFERENCE — A replacement would need national-scale offline navigation, parcel and access data, activity-specific maps, current official restrictions, and claim-level provenance in one coherent product. Ohvernight could only approach this by first owning a narrower regional evidence graph and expanding only where source maintenance is sustainable. https://www.onxmaps.com/about https://www.onxmaps.com/tou

- INFERENCE — Its OSM-based trail search and user-track matching provide broad discovery without requiring Gaia to hand-curate every route. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/360046923514-Find-Hikes-on-gaiagps-com

- INFERENCE — Users must understand layers such as OSM, MVUM, public land, and private land and then verify rules elsewhere; that is powerful for expert map users but burdensome for a permission-sensitive trip decision. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003638568-I-want-to-add-or-remove-something-from-a-Map-source https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051309953-How-do-I-find-offroad-trails-and-maps-in-Gaia-GPS

### What they would be hard to beat at, and why

- INFERENCE — Gaia would be hard to beat at map-source breadth and layer composition because its Premium catalogue contains more than 250 sources and overlays. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003524587-What-maps-are-included-in-Gaia-GPS
- INFERENCE — Offline layered mapping is a durable advantage because it depends on licensing, tile infrastructure, device synchronization, and years of product work. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003524547-Membership-Options-Free-Premium-and-Premium-with-Outside
- INFERENCE — The Outside bundle makes Gaia difficult to compete with on subscription breadth when users also value Trailforks and outdoor media. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003524547-Membership-Options-Free-Premium-and-Premium-with-Outside

### What not to copy

- INFERENCE — Do not copy the assumption that users will assemble authoritative answers from many layers themselves. Ohvernight’s opportunity is to perform that evidence work and show the unresolved parts. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003638568-I-want-to-add-or-remove-something-from-a-Map-source
- INFERENCE — Do not build a large catalogue of licensed layers before establishing a small, inspectable source model for Colorado overnight claims. https://blog.gaiagps.com/gaia-gps-is-improving-satellite-imagery-saying-goodbye-to-esri-world-imagery/

### What their users still need another app for

- INFERENCE — Users need official agency and operator pages for current closures, permits, camping restrictions, reservations, and permission. Gaia’s help pages direct users to map sources and search rather than resolving those questions. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051309953-How-do-I-find-offroad-trails-and-maps-in-Gaia-GPS
- INFERENCE — Users may need a dedicated trail-community or guidebook product for highly curated route conditions and local trail politics. Gaia’s own bundle includes Trailforks, which indicates complementary rather than complete coverage. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003524547-Membership-Options-Free-Premium-and-Premium-with-Outside

### What feature could make a user switch

- INFERENCE — A “minimum evidence packet” for every overnight option—operator, rule, activity compatibility, access route, restrictions, effective date, and source link—could attract users who find Gaia’s layer model too manual. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003638568-I-want-to-add-or-remove-something-from-a-Map-source

### What could make a user pay for both

- INFERENCE — Gaia could remain the offline map workspace while Ohvernight becomes the evidence and overnight-selection layer for Colorado trips. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003524547-Membership-Options-Free-Premium-and-Premium-with-Outside

### What could eventually replace them

- INFERENCE — A replacement would need Gaia’s source breadth and offline engine plus an evidence graph that explains which agency or operator supports each claim. A regional product should not attempt that breadth until it can maintain the sources. https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003524587-What-maps-are-included-in-Gaia-GPS

## Recommendation

- INFERENCE — Ohvernight should not compete as another general-purpose map, trail directory, offline navigator, or community review platform.
- INFERENCE — Its defensible wedge is a regional, evidence-first decision layer: choose activities, identify candidate overnight places, connect each claim to the responsible agency/operator, show restrictions before recommendations, distinguish permission from mere existence, and leave unresolved facts explicitly UNKNOWN.
- INFERENCE — Start with Aspen-area and Douglas County use cases where a small set of authoritative agencies and operators can be maintained manually. Record source URL, publisher, retrieved date, effective date when available, claim type, geographic scope, and whether the source expressly grants permission.
- INFERENCE — Link outward rather than copy restricted incumbent data. Use COTREX, Trailforks, CalTopo, onX, AllTrails, Gaia GPS, and FarOut as products users may continue using, not as datasets to ingest without permission. https://trails.colorado.gov/terms https://www.trailforks.com/about/data/ https://caltopo.com/about/terms-of-use
- INFERENCE — The first paid feature should be a trustworthy trip brief or “stay-compatible activity plan,” not another basemap: a compact answer showing selected activities, candidate overnight options, restrictions, access uncertainty, official links, and what must be verified before departure.
- INFERENCE — The strongest long-term replacement opportunity is not to beat incumbents at navigation. It is to become the evidence and decision layer they do not centrally provide for the question: “Where can I stay overnight while I have fun doing the activities I chose?”
