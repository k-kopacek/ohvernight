## 1. The Dyrt

### Evidence and business model

- PRIMARY SOURCE: The Dyrt PRO is listed at $59.99/year as of 2026. The subscription includes offline access, BLM and USFS map layers, road-trip maps, campground discounts, and Dyrt Alerts. [The Dyrt PRO pricing](https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054724311-How-much-does-PRO-cost-What-is-the-renewal-cost)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: The Dyrt says its public-land maps show boundaries that “may allow free and dispersed camping areas”; its BLM and USFS layers are land and map layers, not site-specific permissions. [PRO map layers](https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049651571-What-map-layers-does-The-Dyrt-PRO-have)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: The Dyrt warns that camping found through PRO may be free, lower-cost, private, or public; permits may still be required even when a site is free. [Camping and PRO Maps](https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049651791-Is-all-camping-that-I-find-with-PRO-Maps-free)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: The Dyrt describes its inventory as including public and private campgrounds, RV parks, glamping, free campsites, and millions of user-generated reviews. [2025 press release](https://thedyrt.com/press/the-dyrt-named-to-inc-5000-for-fourth-consecutive-year-as-top-brands-recognize-the-leader-in-all-things-camping/)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: The Dyrt says private-property and campground owners can list and accept bookings with zero commission to the host. [Host listing page](https://thedyrt.com/claim)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: The Dyrt receives affiliate commission from purchases made through some magazine links. [Affiliate partnerships](https://thedyrt.com/magazine/affiliate/)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: The Dyrt reported 2025 partnerships with Toyota, Ford, Jeep, Ram, Verizon, RVshare, Love’s, and Sea to Summit; it also reported integration into 2025 Jeep and Ram Uconnect systems. [2025 press release](https://thedyrt.com/press/the-dyrt-named-to-inc-5000-for-fourth-consecutive-year-as-top-brands-recognize-the-leader-in-all-things-camping/)
- UNKNOWN: No ownership acquisition or strategic ownership change for 2024–2026 was established in the retrieved first-party sources.

### Dispersed-site provenance and legality

- PRIMARY SOURCE: The Dyrt’s dispersed-camping map evidence is primarily land-boundary and map-layer information plus user-generated campground information. [Map layers](https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049651571-What-map-layers-does-The-Dyrt-PRO-have) [2025 press release](https://thedyrt.com/press/the-dyrt-named-to-inc-5000-for-fourth-consecutive-year-as-top-brands-recognize-the-leader-in-all-things-camping/)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: The Dyrt tells users to check before going because some sites require permits even when free. [PRO Maps warning](https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049651791-Is-all-camping-that-I-find-with-PRO-Maps-free)
- INFERENCE: The Dyrt is materially better than a small regional map at discovery breadth, route planning, offline use, review volume, alerting, and consumer distribution. Its own caveat means its public-land layer should not be represented as a legal answer.

### Strategic assessment

- WHAT THEY DO BETTER TODAY: Broad U.S. discovery, large review volume, offline tools, discounts, trip planning, and sold-out-campground alerts. [INFERENCE — pricing](https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054724311-How-much-does-PRO-cost-What-is-the-renewal-cost) [INFERENCE — 2025 press release](https://thedyrt.com/press/the-dyrt-named-to-inc-5000-for-fourth-consecutive-year-as-top-brands-recognize-the-leader-in-all-things-camping/)
- WHAT THEY DO WORSE: Their land layers identify public-land context, but do not establish that a particular road, pullout, parcel, or pin is open to overnight camping. [INFERENCE — map layers](https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049651571-What-map-layers-does-The-Dyrt-PRO-have) [INFERENCE — PRO Maps warning](https://support.thedyrt.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049651791-Is-all-camping-that-I-find-with-PRO-Maps-free)
- WHAT THEY WOULD BE HARD TO BEAT AT: General-purpose U.S. camping discovery and turning a large community into consumer traffic, subscriptions, brand partnerships, and vehicle integrations. [INFERENCE — 2025 press release](https://thedyrt.com/press/the-dyrt-named-to-inc-5000-for-fourth-consecutive-year-as-top-brands-recognize-the-leader-in-all-things-camping/)
- WHAT NOT TO COPY: A map color or land-ownership overlay that users may read as permission.
- WHAT THEIR USERS STILL NEED ANOTHER APP FOR: Exact agency restrictions, current closure orders, activity access, and a source-backed determination for a specific overnight location. [INFERENCE — White River camping rules](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/whiteriver/recreation/camping-cabins?page) [INFERENCE — Aspen restrictions](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/whiteriver/alerts/aspen-rd-occupancy-and-use-prohibitions)
- WHAT FEATURE COULD MAKE A USER SWITCH: A regional answer that shows the controlling agency/operator page, effective dates, restrictions first, and the relationship between chosen activities and legally supportable overnight options.
- WHAT COULD MAKE A USER PAY FOR BOTH: The Dyrt for national discovery, discounts, offline maps, and alerts; Ohvernight for authoritative local legality, activity fit, and current restriction tracking.
- WHAT COULD EVENTUALLY REPLACE THEM: A larger evidence-first network that combines broad inventory with source-level permission records and remains useful offline.

## 2. Hipcamp

### Evidence and business model

- PRIMARY SOURCE: Hipcamp allows hosts to list properties for free and charges a commission on confirmed bookings. [Host page](https://www.hipcamp.com/en-US/host)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Hipcamp’s campground-owner page states a 15% commission on booked reservations and no monthly subscription or software fee, although integrated-property fees may vary. [Campground owners](https://host.hipcamp.com/campground-owners)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campers pay a variable service fee on bookings with a $3 minimum. [Service fee](https://support.hipcamp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025121211-What-is-the-Hipcamp-service-fee)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Hipcamp requires hosts to follow local regulations, zoning, permits, fire, waste, and other applicable rules. [Host permits and government permission](https://support.hipcamp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025120191-Do-I-need-a-permit-or-government-permission-to-host-campers)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Hipcamp says new hosts must verify identity, pass a background check, agree to hosting standards, and undergo internal quality assurance. [Host vetting](https://support.hipcamp.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406047570708-How-are-Hosts-on-Hipcamp-vetted)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Hipcamp’s 2025 company-news page reports expansion to Sun Outdoors resorts and the addition of board member Erik Blachford. [Company news](https://www.hipcamp.com/journal/category/company-news)
- UNKNOWN: No 2024–2026 ownership acquisition was established in the retrieved official sources.

### Dispersed-site provenance and legality

- PRIMARY SOURCE: Hipcamp inventory is based on landowner or operator listings and bookings, not merely crowd-reported public-land pins. [Host page](https://www.hipcamp.com/en-US/host)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Hipcamp’s rules say a booking is temporary access to a property and requires compliance with property rules and applicable laws. [Terms of Use](https://www.hipcamp.com/en-US/terms)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Hipcamp explicitly tells hosts to investigate local regulations and permits rather than treating listing as proof of legal operation. [Host permits and government permission](https://support.hipcamp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025120191-Do-I-need-a-permit-or-government-permission-to-host-campers)
- INFERENCE: Hipcamp is stronger than a regional public-land map for bookable private-land stays, but a Hipcamp listing is not itself a substitute for an agency or local-government permission record.

### Strategic assessment

- WHAT THEY DO BETTER TODAY: Converts private land, farms, campgrounds, cabins, and unusual accommodations into bookable inventory with payments, host tools, insurance, and support. [INFERENCE — host page](https://www.hipcamp.com/en-US/host) [INFERENCE — host vetting](https://support.hipcamp.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406047570708-How-are-Hosts-on-Hipcamp-vetted)
- WHAT THEY DO WORSE: Host compliance is dependent on local laws, zoning, permits, and property rules; the marketplace does not answer whether dispersed public-land camping is permitted. [INFERENCE — host permission guidance](https://support.hipcamp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025120191-Do-I-need-a-permit-or-government-permission-to-host-campers)
- WHAT THEY WOULD BE HARD TO BEAT AT: Private-land supply, host acquisition, booking conversion, payment handling, and unusual overnight experiences.
- WHAT NOT TO COPY: Treating a listed property as proof that every advertised activity, road, fire, wastewater arrangement, or stay duration is lawful.
- WHAT THEIR USERS STILL NEED ANOTHER APP FOR: Public-land camping, agency restrictions, activity access, trail conditions, and a cross-provider comparison of overnight options near a chosen activity.
- WHAT FEATURE COULD MAKE A USER SWITCH: A regional “why this is available” record showing landowner/operator authority, local permit status, agency restrictions, and nearby activity access.
- WHAT COULD MAKE A USER PAY FOR BOTH: Hipcamp for guaranteed private bookings; Ohvernight for legal context, activity planning, and non-Hipcamp alternatives.
- WHAT COULD EVENTUALLY REPLACE THEM: A permission-verified regional marketplace or a federated system that lets operators publish bookable inventory while agencies publish authoritative restrictions.

## 3. Campendium / Roadtrippers

### Evidence, ownership, pricing, and business model

- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campendium offers a free tier with 70,000+ campgrounds, reviews, photos, and free/dispersed-camping search. RV PRO is listed at $59.99/year or $19.99/month. [Campendium membership](https://campendium.com/membership/)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Roadtrippers Premium unlocks paid features in both Roadtrippers and Campendium. [Roadtrippers Premium support](https://support.campendium.com/hc/en-us/articles/27347130251924-What-s-Included-with-Roadtrippers-Premium)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Roadtrippers lists Basic, Pro, and Premium annual tiers at $0, $35.99, $49.99, and $59.99 depending on tier presentation; the Premium tier includes up to 150 stops, offline maps, RV GPS, public-land maps, cell coverage, smoke maps, discounts, and 16,000+ overnight locations. [Roadtrippers RV plans](https://roadtrippers.com/rv/)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Roadpass’s privacy policy identifies Campendium, Inc. and Roadtrippers, LLC as wholly owned subsidiaries or affiliates of Roadpass Digital. [Roadpass privacy policy](https://roadpass.com/privacy-policy/)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Roadpass describes Campendium as a community-verified campsite platform and Roadtrippers as a road-trip planning, booking, and navigation product; it reports more than 38 million trips planned and an AI-powered Autopilot feature. [Roadpass media](https://roadpass.com/media/)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campendium offers free campground listings and paid advertising or partnership opportunities to campground owners. [Campendium campground support](https://support.campendium.com/hc/en-us/articles/12910930565396-I-own-a-campground-how-can-I-work-with-Campendium)
- UNKNOWN: A specific 2024–2026 Roadpass acquisition or ownership transaction was not established from an official source retrieved in this session.

### Dispersed-site provenance and legality

- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campendium describes itself as a community of campers contributing reviews, photos, and information. [Campendium membership](https://campendium.com/membership/) [Roadpass media](https://roadpass.com/media/)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campendium says dispersed locations may require permits, accessibility is not guaranteed, roads change, and users should contact land management directly for maximum-stay limits. [What is dispersed camping?](https://support.campendium.com/hc/en-us/articles/4501226202388-What-is-dispersed-camping)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campendium provides public-land overlays for BLM, National Park, National Forest, and Arizona State Trust Land. [Roadtrippers Premium support](https://support.campendium.com/hc/en-us/articles/27347130251924-What-s-Included-with-Roadtrippers-Premium)
- INFERENCE: Campendium has unusually good RV-specific community context and cautionary language, but its reviews, overlays, and access reports still do not prove present permission for a specific site.

### Strategic assessment

- WHAT THEY DO BETTER TODAY: RV-specific route planning, navigation, cell coverage, public-land overlays, overnight RV parking, community reviews, and a shared paid layer across two products. [INFERENCE — Campendium membership](https://campendium.com/membership/) [INFERENCE — Premium support](https://support.campendium.com/hc/en-us/articles/27347130251924-What-s-Included-with-Roadtrippers-Premium)
- WHAT THEY DO WORSE: The product combines community reports and geographic layers but does not turn those into a current, parcel-specific legal determination. [INFERENCE — dispersed camping guidance](https://support.campendium.com/hc/en-us/articles/4501226202388-What-is-dispersed-camping)
- WHAT THEY WOULD BE HARD TO BEAT AT: RV-safe routing, vehicle constraints, route-scale planning, and a large RV community.
- WHAT NOT TO COPY: Optimize only for RVs or imply that public-land overlays answer overnight permission.
- WHAT THEIR USERS STILL NEED ANOTHER APP FOR: Non-RV tent users, local activity discovery, current agency orders, local trail access, and a permission source tied to the exact overnight location.
- WHAT FEATURE COULD MAKE A USER SWITCH: A route planner that rejects or warns on overnight candidates when the controlling agency page does not support camping, and that links the restriction before the campsite recommendation.
- WHAT COULD MAKE A USER PAY FOR BOTH: Roadpass for navigation and RV logistics; Ohvernight for activity-centered regional decisions and source-backed legality.
- WHAT COULD EVENTUALLY REPLACE THEM: A national or regional product that retains RV routing but makes official permission and restriction provenance first-class data.

## 4. iOverlander

### Evidence, pricing, and business model

- PRIMARY SOURCE: iOverlander says all places and reviews are created by travelers, with more than 300,000 places across 190 countries and more than 1 million check-ins. [About iOverlander](https://ioverlander.com/about)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: iOverlander launched iOverlander 2 in June 2024 and says it operates on a nonprofit model in which subscription income is returned to app operation rather than distributed as owner profit. [About iOverlander](https://ioverlander.com/about) [iOverlander 2](https://ioverlander.com/whats_coming)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: iOverlander lists Free with ads, Pro at $59.99/year, and Unlimited at $99.99/year. Annual plans begin with a seven-day trial. [Subscriptions](https://ioverlander.com/subscriptions)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: iOverlander says monthly prices increased approximately 50% for new subscribers on 2026-01-01, while annual prices stayed unchanged. [iOverlander 2](https://ioverlander.com/whats_coming)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: iOverlander rewards contributors with free annual Unlimited subscriptions based on accepted places, check-ins, photos, and corrections. [Rewards](https://ioverlander.com/rewards)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Some map and location data comes from OpenStreetMap, while places and reviews are traveler-created. [Contributors](https://ioverlander.com/contributors) [About iOverlander](https://ioverlander.com/about)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: iOverlander’s 2025 update added French, German, Portuguese, and Spanish support, new categories, more amenities, offline translations, and manual review of more than 30,000 places. [iOverlander 2](https://ioverlander.com/whats_coming)

### Dispersed-site provenance and legality

- PRIMARY SOURCE: iOverlander says every place was added by a traveler standing there and that the best data comes from travelers on site. [About iOverlander](https://ioverlander.com/about)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: The retrieved official pages did not establish that a traveler-submitted place has agency permission, an active closure check, or an operator authorization.
- INFERENCE: iOverlander is exceptionally strong for current-feeling overlander knowledge, practical conditions, and worldwide coverage, but it is the clearest example of why “someone stayed there” must not become “camping is allowed there.”

### Strategic assessment

- WHAT THEY DO BETTER TODAY: Global coverage, traveler presence, offline use, overlander-specific categories, contributor incentives, translations, and practical reports. [INFERENCE — about page](https://ioverlander.com/about) [INFERENCE — 2025 update](https://ioverlander.com/whats_coming)
- WHAT THEY DO WORSE: Provenance is usually traveler-based rather than agency-based; the user must infer legality from reports, and the product does not establish permission merely because a place exists.
- WHAT THEY WOULD BE HARD TO BEAT AT: Global overlanding breadth and the density of practical place reports.
- WHAT NOT TO COPY: A large crowd database without a visible separation between “reported,” “verified,” “agency-authorized,” “operator-authorized,” and “unknown.”
- WHAT THEIR USERS STILL NEED ANOTHER APP FOR: Agency restrictions, fire and seasonal orders, permitted activity access, current local rules, and trustworthy regional trip planning.
- WHAT FEATURE COULD MAKE A USER SWITCH: A “community report plus official status” view that preserves traveler notes while showing the controlling agency/operator source and its effective date.
- WHAT COULD MAKE A USER PAY FOR BOTH: iOverlander for worldwide offline overlanding; Ohvernight for Colorado-specific legal and activity context.
- WHAT COULD EVENTUALLY REPLACE THEM: A federated community system with global reports but local evidence maintainers and explicit permission states.

## 7. Campnab

### Evidence, pricing, and business model

- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campnab offers monthly memberships at $10, $20, and $30; annual plans at $90, $180, and $270; and pay-per-use scans at $10, $15, and $20. [Campnab pricing](https://campnab.com/pricing)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campnab says it monitors approximately 3,200 parks and 7,000 campgrounds across Canada and the United States, plus permits and tickets in some systems. [Campnab pricing](https://campnab.com/pricing)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campnab says it does not book a campsite; it sends an alert and the user must complete the reservation through the park’s site. [Campnab homepage](https://campnab.com/)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campnab says it is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or officially connected with the parks it scans. [Campnab FAQ](https://campnab.com/faq)
- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campnab identifies an affiliate program as an additional revenue channel. [Campnab FAQ](https://campnab.com/faq)
- UNKNOWN: Ownership or a 2024–2026 strategic move was not established.

### Dispersed-site provenance and legality

- PRIMARY SOURCE: Campnab monitors availability in park reservation systems; its FAQ says the service does not control supply and cannot guarantee an alert or booking. [Campnab FAQ](https://campnab.com/faq)
- INFERENCE: Campnab data is inventory-derived, not permission-derived. A reservation opening can establish only that the booking system reported availability, not that every desired activity or access route is open.

### Strategic assessment

- WHAT THEY DO BETTER TODAY: Focused cancellation monitoring, many scan criteria, multiple pricing tiers, SMS alerts, and human support. [INFERENCE — pricing](https://campnab.com/pricing) [INFERENCE — FAQ](https://campnab.com/faq)
- WHAT THEY DO WORSE: No trip planning, activity linkage, legal interpretation, or booking completion.
- WHAT THEY WOULD BE HARD TO BEAT AT: A narrow, well-understood sold-out-campground alert workflow.
- WHAT NOT TO COPY: Charging for an alert without making the no-guarantee and booking handoff prominent.

## Recommendation

Build Ohvernight as an evidence-first regional decision layer, not as another national campground directory or cancellation-alert clone.

Prioritize:

1. A restriction-first record for every overnight candidate:
   - controlling agency, operator, or landowner;
   - exact source URL;
   - retrieval date;
   - geographic scope;
   - effective date and expiry or rescission condition;
   - stay limit, fire rule, permit requirement, seasonal closure, and designated-site rule.

2. Separate statuses:
   - permission evidenced;
   - reservation available;
   - access reported;
   - activity access evidenced;
   - restriction active;
   - community report;
   - unknown.

3. Never display “allowed” unless a current agency or operator sentence supports that exact location and use.

4. Make the user’s activity choice the starting point:
   - select hiking, climbing, paddling, fishing, biking, skiing, or another activity;
   - show overnight options only after checking the relevant activity and overnight evidence;
   - show the authoritative booking, permit, restriction, or operator page beside the result.

5. Use competitors selectively:
   - Recreation.gov for official federal reservations and permits;
   - Campflare or Campnab for optional availability alerts;
   - The Dyrt, Campendium, iOverlander, and Sēkr for discovery and community context;
   - Hipcamp and Harvest Hosts for private inventory;
   - never treat any of those layers as a replacement for the controlling agency or operator source.

6. Make the product worth paying for through confidence, not volume:
   - maintained Colorado source records;
   - reliable restriction alerts;
   - activity-to-overnight matching;
   - offline evidence packets for low-service areas;
   - transparent unknowns and source history.
