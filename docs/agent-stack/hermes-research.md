Select only assigned sections. Results are research inputs, not approved changes to products, trust policy or source scope.

## Source hierarchy and trust

1. For a claim, use the responsible agency/operator's own rules, official orders, primary publications and source metadata. Check publisher authority, jurisdiction, spatial applicability, dates, conflicts and activity scope.
2. For technical semantics, use publisher documentation, dictionaries, coded-value domains and bounded sample records. For licensing, use the actual license/terms and publisher metadata, not a search snippet or secondary assertion.
3. For market facts, use official pricing, product/help documentation and release notes. Compare observed workflows carefully; record plan, country/currency and date for prices.
4. Use secondary reporting for leads and context, clearly attributed. Community discussions/reviews are customer-pain signals only; they never establish legal permission, access, ownership or current closure status.

Unknown remains unknown. Ownership ≠ access; mapped existence/proximity ≠ permission; retrieval ≠ confirmation. Positive claims fail closed. A ramp, stocking, public-land label or recreation designation does not establish fishing, paddling, boating, swimming or access permission. Stale evidence never weakens a restriction. Absence of a prohibition is not evidence of allowance.

For M4, preserve the four evidence classes in the master contract. Draft fishing, boating, paddling and swimming interpretations independently. Do not propose `access: allowed`; M4 leaves access unknown unless the same operator page supports the `restricted` access status specified in the approved registry rules. Do not widen that vocabulary. M4 has no community records or community ingestion. Source conflict or incomplete positive evidence stays unresolved/unknown with the restriction retained for review.

## Licensing and terms checks

For every candidate dataset/service, separately record rights to access/fetch, scrape, transform, cache/store, publish, redistribute, use commercially and make derivatives; attribution/share-alike obligations, rate limits, retention, privacy and termination conditions. Cite the controlling text/page and retrieval date; distinguish explicit permission, explicit prohibition and unclear/no statement. An accessible endpoint, government publisher or downloadable file does not imply unrestricted reuse.

Use public metadata and minimal authorized samples; do not bypass login, paywalls, rate limits or terms. Unclear/prohibitive rights lock the intended use; report the exact uncertainty and a proposed clarification question. Prepare outreach drafts, but do not send messages, contact agencies/customers, purchase access or accept new obligations without explicit owner authorization. CPW structured persistence remains gated; M4-A/B/C do not wait for M4-D.

## Evidence dossier format

One dossier per water/source/question, with:

- Stable dossier ID; feature/candidate ID and input SHA if supplied; place/jurisdiction and exact question.
- Publisher, authority, source URL/title, source publication/effective date where available, UTC retrieval timestamp and relevant section/page.
- Minimal decisive excerpt with location, and a separate interpretation. Honor reuse/copyright limits; do not copy whole pages or third-party review collections into the repo.
- Evidence class and fact dimension. For activity dossiers, independent status proposals (`allowed`, `prohibited`, `restricted`, `unknown`), conditions/seasonality, closure conflicts and what remains unknown.
- Rights/terms conclusion and supporting links; permitted intended use vs unresolved use.
- Confidence in the interpretation, spatial/temporal limitations, contradictory evidence and whether a newer authoritative restriction exists.
- Required coordinator re-read, owner decision and next verification step. Clearly mark `draft research`; do not set production `last_confirmed_at` from retrieval.

Claude must re-read claim-bearing sources; the owner approves each production non-unknown record under the spec. Hermes cannot certify a region/layer as verified, open, permitted or complete. Supply source/check timestamps without claiming confirmation.

## Market and competitor analysis

Compare AllTrails, onX/onX Offroad/onX Hunt, COTREX, camping/dispersed-camping resources, land/access tools, water/fishing resources and other assigned competitors using current primary evidence. For each: target user/job, domains covered, strongest features/data, trail/water/overnight integration, offline capability, planning, vehicle constraints, free/paid boundary, current pricing/source/date, restrictions/evidence presentation, weaknesses and missing workflow links. Distinguish a documented feature, an observed feature and an untested assumption.

## Complaint mining and app-switching workflows

Use a bounded public sample of user complaints/reviews/support discussions where viewing is permitted. Document platform/date range/sample size/search method, repeated pain categories, counterexamples, selection bias and confidence. Link to examples and paraphrase; no bulk scraping/storage, personal-contact lists or community content in the production pipeline. Frequency in this sample is not market prevalence.

Map a representative workflow: trigger → current apps/steps → evidence/permissions the user must verify → friction/failure → workaround → proposed Ohvernight experience. Look for fragmented activity-plus-overnight planning, unclear rules, source freshness, vehicle/setup mismatch and offline planning needs. Preserve uncertainty instead of proposing a feature that “solves” missing legal evidence by inference.

## Idea fairy: monetizable opportunities and first customer

Generate evidence-backed product opportunities rather than random brainstorming. Each opportunity card includes:

1. Target user and concrete job/trigger; repeated complaint and competitor evidence.
2. Current workflow/apps, costs and practical failure; smallest useful Ohvernight workflow.
3. Data needed, what exists, missing rights/coverage/freshness, trust risks and failure boundaries.
4. Why someone might pay, existing substitute/spend, what would make them cancel and counterevidence. A hypothesis is not demonstrated willingness to pay.
5. First paying customer hypothesis: a specific segment, use moment, acquisition channel, offer and price hypothesis, with evidence and uncertainty. Do not invent a customer, interview or payment.
6. Smallest validation experiment, measurable success/failure criteria, rough effort and milestone fit; identify owner decisions before building/contacting/spending.
7. Bear case, likely imitation/substitutes, defensibility and recommendation to validate/defer/reject.

Research possible paid planning workflows while preserving Explore's free-discovery role. Pricing, paywalls, billing, business-model changes and new features are owner decisions. A recommendation cannot silently become the roadmap.

## Bear case and moat analysis

Argue the strongest case against each major opportunity: free substitutes, low frequency, unreliable evidence, source/licensing costs, weak willingness to pay, incumbents adding it, acquisition/retention burden and complexity. State which finding would falsify the recommendation.

Assess defensibility through lawful cross-domain data/evidence integration, quality/freshness operations, workflow completion, distribution and first-party data collected with proper consent. Public data alone is not a moat. Distinguish compounding advantages from easily copied features and from aspirations requiring rights or scale not held today. Do not recommend certainty inflation or hidden safety facts as monetization.

## Search bounds, models and stops

Use Hermes+Luna for broad extraction/surveys and straightforward terms extraction; Hermes+Sol for ambiguous terms/source semantics, sensitive dossier interpretation and high-value synthesis. Switching models does not resolve rights or owner gates, and may still consume the same pool.

Unless the package specifies stricter caps, each focused section allows at most **10 searches / 40 tool calls**, and a broad section **25 searches / 70 tool calls**, with a **60-minute** section budget. State a finite total mission budget before starting. Use exact starting URLs, cache a visited-source index and never repeat an identical query. Rephrase once at most. After two failed access methods for a source, mark inaccessible and use another authorized source. Never run an unbounded search loop.

At a cap, write findings/unknowns and advance to the next independent section if mission budget/reserve permits. Stop an affected section for contradictory authoritative restrictions, terms that block use, only community evidence for a legal claim, external contact/spend required, unreliable applicability or a repeated hang. Stop the mission at its total cap, protected reserve, explicit stop or documented saturation. Missing official evidence is a valid result.
